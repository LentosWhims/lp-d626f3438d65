Reading the year-on-year numbers

  • Weekly is the one to watch. Weeks are matched 364 days back, so every comparison is Monday-to-Monday against the same seven weekdays. Daily comparisons are dominated by day-of-week swings; monthly ones are distorted by how many weekends a month happens to contain.
  • Monthly is for trend, not precision. Use the per-day average column rather than the raw total when month lengths differ.
  • Average ticket is the cleanest year-on-year signal of all — it is a ratio, so it is immune to period length, weekday mix and footfall. Revenue up with ticket flat means more orders; revenue up with ticket up means customers are spending more per visit. It counts store POS revenue only, since machine and vending takings have no order attached.
  • Daily is for spotting incidents — a machine down, a closure — not for judging performance. It is matched to the same weekday a year earlier, not the same date.
  • Anything compared against before 2025-05-09 is the previous owner's trading, not yours. Those rows are marked “prev. owner”; a comparison span that straddles the handover is marked “crosses acq.”.
  • On the change chart, a bar with an arrowhead has been clipped — the scale is fitted to the bulk of the data so the early ramp-up doesn't flatten everything else. Hover for the true figure.
  • The POS changed on 2026-04-04, with both systems running to 2026-05-15. Combined revenue stays comparable across that boundary; the per-source split does not.
Revenue = self-serve machine cycles + vending + store POS (in-store and pickup & delivery), plus the retired legacy POS for periods before the cutover. Store POS figures use the collected basis. Sums, averages and year-on-year deltas are derived from the stored daily readings. Incomplete periods are compared like for like — the same number of elapsed days on both sides.
